SECTOR SIGNALS & TURNOVER · RESEARCH EXTENSION

Does trading less preserve the signal?

Eleven sector ETFs, two reversal signals, one fixed rank buffer. The question is whether fewer replacements save enough trading cost to offset any change in gross P&L.

2026: lower turnover, lower net P&L for both buffered policies.
Turnover fell 24.4–27.3%, while buffered minus fresh net P&L was raw −$10,465; market-adjusted −$3,451. The improvement seen in 2022–2025 did not carry into this 41-week historical period. No parameter was retuned after seeing it.

2019–2021 and 2022–2025 are previously observed, exploratory periods. The 2026 snapshot was acquired after the extension specification; it is historical evidence, not a prospective live test.

The rule, in one example

Fresh selection buys the lowest three scores and shorts the highest three every week. The buffer keeps an existing long at ranks 1–5 and an existing short at ranks 7–11, then fills vacant slots from the extremes. Each side still holds three ETFs at ±1/6 weight.

A long moves from rank 3 to rank 5: keep it. At rank 6, replace it.
A short is at rank 7: keep it. At rank 6, replace it. Score ties use alphabetical symbols.

Raw scores use five-session returns; market-adjusted scores subtract the sector’s estimated beta times the SPY move. Betas use 126 earlier daily returns ending before the signal window. The buffer changes membership, not either signal. All four variants were specified in advance of acquiring this 2026 snapshot.

Accounting, uncertainty and source files

Holdings start flat separately in each of the three periods. Every weekly entry rebalances the selected six names to the same target dollar exposure, including drift trades when membership is unchanged; final liquidation also incurs cost. Fixed gross exposure does not imply fixed risk or beta neutrality. Annual summaries, replacements, basket beta, historical volatility and rank persistence are in the linked files.

The ex-post 2026 crossover one-way costs are Raw 67.1 bp; Market-adjusted 28.7 bp. These solve the linear cost accounting identity; they are not recommended trading costs or optimized settings.

2019–2021 and 2022–2025 were already observed and are exploratory for this extension. 2026 prices were acquired after the extension specification, but remain historical data, not a prospective live test or globally unseen history. Earlier periods retain their original snapshots; the 2026 run uses its own internally consistent snapshot with prior history for signals. Price levels are not spliced.

Adjusted opens are total-return proxies rather than executable fills. Vendor revisions, changing ETF holdings, unavailable spreads and depth, assumed borrow, and simplified distribution and funding accounting remain limitations.
